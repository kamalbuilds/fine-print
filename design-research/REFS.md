## ToS;DR service page (direct competitor)

- **Screen:** product screen, the Facebook service page at tosdr.org/en/service/182. PNGs: `design-research/refs/tosdr-1440.png`, `design-research/refs/tosdr-390.png`.
- **Measured:** dark page, body text rgb(224,224,224). Primary face is the system stack (`-apple-system, system-ui, segoe ui, Roboto`). h1 32px/700, leading 51.2px; h2 "Points" 24px/700; each point row 16px/700, leading 22.4px, coloured by severity (bad rows rgb(248,113,113) on a dark red band, warnings amber, good green, neutral grey). Grade chip "Grade E" pinned top right of the header card.
- **Steal:** the verdict lives in the colour of the row before you read a word, and the grade is one glanceable token. Severity ordering (bad first, then warnings, then good, then neutral) is the right reading order for a worried user.
- **Numbers, empty, action:** no counts at all; 70+ rows of equal weight; no primary action on the page except Donate. Points carry no quote on the list, the evidence is two clicks away behind each row's icons.
- **Do not take:** 70 equal rows (a wall nobody finishes), system-ui type, colour as the only severity signal, evidence hidden behind icons. Fine Print shows six findings and the quote beside each claim.

- **Do not take:** Inter, the hand-maintained data ("Last Updated May 26, 2021" on the screen captured in 2026), the collapsed chevron rows that hide the evidence, colour dots as the only signal. Fine Print reads the policy live and puts the exact sentence on the page.

## App Store privacy label, Strava (direct alternative a user already has)

- **Screen:** product screen, apps.apple.com Strava listing, "App Privacy" section. PNGs: `design-research/refs/applelabel-1440.png`, `design-research/refs/applelabel-390.png`.
- **Measured:** dark page, ink rgba(255,255,255,0.92). SF Pro via `-apple-system`. h1 app name 34px/700, tracking -0.68px, leading 40px; section heads ("App Privacy", "Ratings & Reviews") 17px/700; body 15-17px/400. Privacy section is three equal dark cards about 170px wide each at 1440, radius about 12px, each with a 20px icon, a 13px/600 title ("Data Used to Track You", "Data Linked to You", "Data Not Linked to You") and a two-column list of data categories with 14px glyphs.
- **Steal:** the three-way split by consequence (tracks you / linked to you / not linked) is the clearest mental model in the category; a category list with an icon per data type scans in two seconds.
- **Numbers, empty, action:** no figures; the label opens with "The developer, Strava, Inc., indicated..." and links to "developer's privacy policy". It is self-reported, never checked, and sits two-thirds down a store page under screenshots, events and reviews.
- **Do not take:** self-reported claims presented as fact, the buried placement, three equal cards as the whole story. Fine Print states who said it (the policy itself, quoted) and checks it.

## Genius lyrics page with annotations (outside domain: text with its explanation beside it)

- **Measured:** Programme (loaded) over Arial, the whole page in one family. h1 song title "Programme Alt" 32px/400, leading 36px, white on a brown-to-black hero gradient (bottom edge rgb(70,46,23)). Lyrics 16px Programme on white, leading 23.2px, ink rgb(0,0,0); every annotated line carries a flat grey background rgb(233,233,233) that hugs the text run, radius 0px, no border. Meta row (contributors, translations) 12px/100, tracking 0.4px; nav and "Sign Up" 12px/100, tracking 1px. - **Numbers, empty, action:** figures are small meta ("12.4M Views", "1067 Contributors") in 12px under the title, never a hero stat. Empty state is the unclicked rail, which Genius fills with recommendations (a wasted slot). Primary action is implicit: click a highlighted line. At 390 the hero eats the first screen and the lyrics start below the fold.
- **Do not take:** click-to-reveal (Fine Print shows claim and quote together, no hidden layer), the one-weight-100 everything, ad slots in the rail, the hero that pushes content off the mobile first screen.

## GitHub pull request, Files changed (outside domain: before/after with a verdict per line)

- **Screen:** product screen, github.com/expressjs/express/pull/5555/files, split diff, dark theme (signed-in session). PNGs: `design-research/refs/githubdiff-1440.png`, `design-research/refs/githubdiff-390.png`.
- **Measured:** Mona Sans VF over `-apple-system` stack; page bg rgb(13,17,23), ink rgb(240,246,252), muted rgb(145,152,161). h1 PR title 32px/600, leading 40px, tracking normal; file headers (h3) 14px/400, leading 21px; body 14px/21px; buttons 14px/500, radius 6px. Diff in monospace about 12px. Removed-line gutter rgb(79,40,40) with the line body rgb(36,25,28); added-line gutter rgb(40,67,43), body rgb(19,28,26), and the exact changed token ("String(url)") gets a stronger inline wash rgb(47,87,50). "Merged" pill rgb(130,89,221), fully rounded. Review comment card sits inline under line R908, header "Comment on line R908" 12px.
- **Steal:** two strengths of mark. The whole line gets a faint tint, the exact words that matter get a brighter wash inside it. Fine Print can do the same: the quote row is tinted, the matched span inside the policy text is the bright mark, and a struck quote reads like a deleted line (red gutter, strikethrough), a language every developer already reads without a legend. Also: the comment is anchored to a named line ("Comment on line R908"), so every claim cites where it came from.
- **Numbers, empty, action:** counts are small and functional ("+15", five-block change bar, "0 / 3 viewed", tab badges "3", "1", "0"); no hero figure. Unchanged regions collapse to a hunk header "@@ -905,7 +905,7 @@" with an expand control, which is the empty state for "nothing here". Primary actions are per item ("Viewed" checkbox, "Resolve comment").
- **Do not take:** the split diff at 390 (code wraps to one word a line and the comment card collapses to a 40px sliver), chrome density (two nav bars, file tree, onboarding popover over content), monospace for prose. Fine Print stacks claim over quote on mobile.

## Our World in Data chart page (outside domain: a figure that carries its own provenance)

- **Screen:** product screen, ourworldindata.org/grapher/life-expectancy. PNGs: `design-research/refs/owid-1440.png`, `design-research/refs/owid-390.png`.
- **Measured:** two faces. Playfair Display for titles: chart h1 "Life expectancy" 25px/600, leading 30px, ink rgb(45,46,45); related-article h3 18px/700, navy rgb(29,61,99). Lato for everything else: body 16px/400, leading 25.6px, navy rgb(29,61,99); chart subtitle 15px/400 grey rgb(91,91,91); control labels 12px/900 uppercase, tracking 1.2px, grey rgb(118,118,118). Nav bar navy rgb(9,32,68) closed by a 3px red rule rgb(189,56,43). Metadata panel under the chart a flat blue-grey rgb(241,244,249), radius 0px, no border. Buttons 14px/500, radius 0px.
- **Steal:** provenance is part of the figure, not a footer. Directly under the plot: "Data source: Riley (2005); Zijdeman et al. (2015); HMD (2025); UNWPP (2024)" with each name a link, then licence "CC BY", then download/share. The panel below repeats it as label-over-value pairs ("Last updated 2025-10-22", "Next expected update 2026-12-11", "Managed by"). The term in the subtitle ("Period life expectancy") is dotted-underlined and glossed in the same sentence. For Fine Print: every finding ends in a source line (policy URL, fetched-at time, "quote found on page" check), set small and quiet like this.
- **Numbers, empty, action:** numbers sit on the axis with units in words ("80 years"); the timeline slider "1770 to 2023" is the main control. Unloaded related charts render as flat grey blocks rgb(241,244,249), a calm skeleton instead of spinners. Primary action is quiet ("Download options", "Edit countries"); Donate is the only filled red button.
- **Do not take:** at 1440 the grapher rendered into a 200px-wide column inside a large white field (a capture of a responsive bug, the chart wants its full width); Playfair Display (a common editorial default); the related-charts wall below. Take the source line, not the page.

## Tufte CSS (outside domain: the note sits on the same line as the sentence it explains)

- **Screen:** article page, edwardtufte.github.io/tufte-css, rendered in its dark scheme by the profile's colour preference. PNGs: `design-research/refs/tufte-1440.png`, `design-research/refs/tufte-390.png`.
- **Measured:** one family, et-book (loaded) falling back to Palatino and Georgia. Root 15px; running text 21px/400, leading 30px; h1 48px/400, leading 48px; h2 33px/400 italic; h3 25.5px/400 italic; author line 27px italic; tracking normal everywhere, weight 400 everywhere (hierarchy by size and italic only). Dark scheme: bg rgb(21,21,21), ink rgb(221,221,221); light scheme in its CSS is #fffff8 on #111111. Links keep body colour with an underline. At 1440 the text column runs x=180 to about x=870 (about 690px, roughly 55% of width); sidenotes start at x=942 in a column about 350px wide, set smaller (about 16px) with a superscript numeral that matches the one in the sentence.
- **Steal:** the margin note is placed at the exact vertical position of the line that references it, so the eye moves sideways, not down. For Fine Print, the plain-English claim and its verbatim quote share a top edge: claim in the main column, quote in the margin column (or the reverse), never a tooltip or footnote at the bottom. Hierarchy with one weight, size and italic only, which leaves bold free to mark the matched words inside a quote.
- **Numbers, empty, action:** no figures, no actions; numbered sidenotes (superscript "2") are the only counter. Unnumbered margin notes carry asides. Empty margin is left empty, which reads as calm rather than broken.
- **Do not take:** the book-page cosplay (et-book with italic heads is the most copied "tasteful" web look), 48px titles with no negative tracking, the long single-column essay. At 390 the margin collapses and notes become tap-to-expand toggles, so the side-by-side argument disappears; Fine Print must stack claim directly above quote on mobile instead.

Brand yellow rgb(251,212,88) on "Snopes Membership", the slanted "Fact Check" flag and author pill. ## Wikipedia article, GDPR (outside domain: every claim carries a numbered citation inline)

- **Screen:** product screen, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Data_Protection_Regulation, Vector 2022 skin. PNGs: `design-research/refs/wikipedia-1440.png`, `design-research/refs/wikipedia-390.png`.
- **Measured:** h1 "Linux Libertine", Georgia serif at 28.8px/400, leading 39.6px, ink rgb(16,20,24), with a 1px rule under it; body sans-serif 16px/400, leading 26px, ink rgb(32,33,34); links rgb(51,102,204). Article column about 560px at 1440 beside a 330px infobox with a lavender section band (History, Preparative texts); left contents rail about 170px.
- **Steal:** the citation is a superscript `[1]` sitting on the exact clause it supports, so trust attaches to a sentence, not to the page. The infobox is a ruled two-column key/value table (Title, Made by, Date made) that answers "what is this, who made it, when" in one glance; Fine Print's provenance rail should take that shape: policy URL, read at, length, reading level.
- **Numbers, empty, action:** dates and identifiers set plain in the value column, no emphasis; no primary action besides search; "citation needed" is the empty state for an unsupported claim, which is exactly Fine Print's struck-through quote.
- **Do not take:** the blue-link density (every third word underlined), the serif/sans mismatch at 28.8px with normal tracking, three rails at once. One rail only.
