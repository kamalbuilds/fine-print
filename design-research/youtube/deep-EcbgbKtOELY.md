## Techniques, with timestamps

- **0:00 Signifiers.** "if I put a container around drinks, you know that it's selected ... if I gray this text out, you know that it's inactive". State comes from container and tone, not from labels.
- **1:01 Hierarchy is contrast.** "it's this contrast, the difference between small and big, or colorful and not that actually creates the hierarchy". Price goes "top, right aligned, and blue" because it is different from everything else.
- **1:33 Draw the relationship.** "we can use icons and a line to demonstrate that it's moving from Jamesville to Syracuse without having to actually say that".
- **2:05 Grid is for repeating content.** Grids are "useful for responsiveness" on "galleries, blogs or any repeating content"; whitespace matters more.
- **2:35 One gap, then group.** "simply 32 pixels between every item, but you can also group elements that just go together". Grouping is a form of hierarchy.
- **3:05 Four-point grid.** "Everything is a multiple, not because it inherently looks better, but because you can always split things in half".
- **3:35 Display type.** "tighten up the letter spacing on this by about -2 [to] -3% and drop the line height to about 110 to 120%".
- **4:05 Size budget.** "you generally don't want more than six font sizes"; dashboards: "you don't normally have text sizes larger than 24 pixels because of the increase in information density".
- **5:36 Semantic colour only.** "Blue for trust, red for danger or urgency, yellow for warning, and green for success. Make sure you use color for purpose, not just for decoration."
- **6:06 Shadows.** "reducing the opacity and dialing up the blur"; "if the shadow is the first thing you notice on a design, you're not using it right".
- **6:37 Icon size equals line height.** "get the line height of your font, in this case 24 pixels, and make the icons the same size".
- **7:08 Every action gets a response.** "every button needs at least four states: default, hovered, active, or pressed, and disabled"; inputs need focus and error states.
- **8:09 Micro-interaction confirms.** On copy: "if we have this chip slide up, that's a micro interaction that confirms our action".

## What applies to Fine Print

- The verified/struck state of a quote is a signifier. Render it with container and tone (a struck quote greys out and loses its container), so nobody needs a legend.
- Draw the claim-to-quote relationship (a rule or connector across the diptych gutter) rather than writing "Source:".
- The gateway side panel is dashboard density, so cap its type at 24px and use the four-point grid. The diptych can carry the one big size.
- Colour budget: one neutral ramp plus semantic red (quote not found) and one verified tone. No decorative accent.
- Copying a quote needs the slide-up confirm chip. Loading the six findings needs a visible loading state per diptych.
