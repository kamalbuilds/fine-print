A before/after redesign of a vibe-coded URL shortener. Sponsor segment (Builder.io) runs 2:33 to 3:04.

## Techniques, with timestamps

- **0:00 Emojis out.** "Get rid of the emojis ... if we take this card and just swap to some more professional icons, it already starts looking better." Phosphor or Lucide.
- **0:30 Colour.** "never let an AI choose any of your colors. They always go for bright colors that don't really work well together."
- **1:01 Repeated KPIs are a tell.** "these four KPIs at the top show up not once, not twice, but three times in a small app. Bringing me to my next point. Never let an AI choose your layout either."
- **1:31 Generated avatars are a tell.** "AI also loves to make these gradient profile circles with letters in them."
- **1:31 Collapse noise.** Collapse buttons "into a triple dot menu", "Collapse these chips to just icons".
- **3:04 Dead cards.** "this card doesn't do anything. And that's a pattern you're going to see a lot with AI, so it can go."
- **4:07 Size by what users care about.** "Drop the size of the name since no one cares about that. Increase the size of the cost per month since people do care about that."
- **5:38 Show the product.** On the landing page, replace stock icons with "a slightly edited image of the analytics page we designed".

## What applies to Fine Print

- The gateway side panel is where AI-dashboard tells creep in. Do not show KPI tiles for tokens, cost and latency. Show one line per tool call, with cost aligned in a right-hand tabular-numeral column.
- Size by what the reader cares about: the claim and the verdict on the quote are big. The model name and request id are small.
- Delete any element that does nothing: no decorative stat cards, no avatar circle, no emoji severity markers.
- The landing state (before a product is entered) should show a real verified diptych from a real policy, not icons describing the feature.
