## Techniques, with timestamps

- **0:34 Data into information.** "transform raw data into visually organized information that conveys a message quickly"; "it's not a word document it's not just plain text or raw data it's an infographic that kind of tells a story".
- **2:41 Three to five levels.** "you want to find some [sweet] spot between three and maximum five", following the magazine cover model: "a visual hook as the primary focus ... smaller subheadings as the secondary focus and then article text for supporting details".
- **4:46 Visual direction.** "through the use of colors hierarchies textures shapes and [white] space you can direct the eye". He shows a broken journey at 7:29, where a phrase is split by an illustration.
- **8:34 Balance is equalized tension.** It "is not just having equal weights in both sides"; alternating "text image image text" "really becomes super boring".
- **11:12 Clarity of intention.** "either be bold or be really really [subtle] but avoid [grey] areas". A title at "164 pixels" makes the intent clear, while "20 pixels here and 14 pixels here ... doesn't really work": "either make it double or more like the next hierarchy or don't change the hierarchy".
- **13:53 Contrast by case.** To separate nav from paragraph, "the nav items and the ctas all caps and smaller ... look like a block".
- **16:36 Repeat every decision.** "you want to repeat every visual decision that you make at least three times".
- **24:11 Cap-height trim.** "you're not using the standard vertical Trim in figma now they have a cap height to Baseline that's what you want to always be using".
- **26:22 Align with numbers, not by eye.** "if it says mixed they're not properly aligned".
- **28:01 Loose on one edge.** Paragraphs get exact column widths. CTAs and images can align on one edge only.

## What applies to Fine Print

- The page is data into information. Six findings are not a bulleted list. Each diptych is a small infographic: claim, quote and verdict mark.
- Three levels only: claim (hook), quote (support), meta (section number, verdict word, gateway lines). No fourth size.
- Size steps must at least double or not exist. If the claim is 32px, the quote sits at 16px. Never 18 against 16.
- Use small caps or uppercase tracking for meta labels (FINDING 03, NOT IN POLICY), so meta reads as a block against sentence-case prose.
- Repeat the axis rule, the verdict mark and the numbering in all six findings. That repetition is what makes them read as a designed system.
- Use CSS `text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic` (or the equivalent) so the claim and quote tops align optically across the axis.
